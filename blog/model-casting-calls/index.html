Model Casting Calls: What Really Happens, From the People Who Run Them

By Online Model Academy · The people who book models · August 2026 · 10 min read

Most articles about model casting calls are written by people who have never sat behind the casting table. At Online Model Academy, our instructors are casting directors and agents, the people who decide who gets booked. So this is not a list of vague tips. It is a walkthrough of what a casting call actually is, how to find real ones, what happens in the room minute by minute, and what we are genuinely deciding when you walk in. The models who understand the process book more work than the models who simply show up and hope.

What Is a Model Casting Call?

A model casting call is an in-person or virtual audition where a client, brand, designer, or agency evaluates models for a specific job or for representation. From the booking side it always means the same thing: someone has a budget, a creative vision, and a gap that needs a face, a body, or a walk to fill it.

Here is what a lot of new models miss. A casting is not a beauty contest. By the time it happens, the client already knows roughly what they want. Our job as casting directors is to find the person who fits the brief, works well on set, and will not create problems for the production. You are not being judged against some universal standard of beauty. You are being measured against a brief you have never seen. That single insight should change how you handle rejection, and we will come back to it.

The Three Main Types of Casting Calls

Open calls. Anyone can attend, no invitation needed. Agencies hold open calls to scout new faces, and occasionally brands hold them for large campaigns or fashion week. Expect a line and a short meeting. Many working models started at an open call, but the realistic goal is to get remembered and called back, not booked on the spot.

Requested castings (go-sees). Invitation-only. The client or casting director has seen your digitals, comp card, or agency package and asked to meet you; if you are represented, your booker sends the details. You have already passed the first filter, so expectations are higher: show up prepared, on time, and matching the photos that got you requested.

Virtual and self-tape castings. Increasingly common, especially for e-commerce, commercial, and out-of-town clients. You film yourself following a brief, usually a slate, turns, and a walk, then submit digitally. Self-tapes are now often the first round even for jobs that hold in-person callbacks; we cover them properly further down.

How to Find Legitimate Model Casting Calls

Real castings flow through a few predictable channels:

  • Agencies. If you are signed, your agency sends you castings. This is the single biggest reason representation matters. If you are not signed yet, our guide on how to get signed by a modeling agency walks through exactly what agencies look for.
  • Agency open calls. Most legitimate agencies list open call hours or digital submission instructions on their websites, free of charge.
  • Reputable casting platforms. Established platforms that verify the productions posting on them can be a genuine source of commercial and print work.
  • Casting directors' official channels. Many casting offices post open submissions on their verified social accounts, particularly around fashion week.
  • Direct brand announcements. Brands sometimes run open castings for specific campaigns on their own official channels.

How to Smell a Fake Casting Call

Scams targeting aspiring models all rely on the same thing: your eagerness. Here is how to spot them.

You are asked to pay. Legitimate castings never charge you to attend, to be "considered," or to be placed on a roster. Real agencies earn commission from the work they book you, not from up-front fees. Any "casting" that leads to a mandatory paid photoshoot, paid classes as a condition of booking, or a registration fee is a sales funnel, not a casting.

The details are vague. Real castings name the client or project type, the usage, the dates, and the location. "Major brand seeking fresh faces, DM for details" is not how professionals communicate.

The location is wrong. Real castings happen in studios, casting offices, agency offices, or production spaces. A casting at a private residence, or one that keeps moving, is a walk-away.

Instant flattery and pressure. "You have exactly the look we need, but we need a decision today." Real casting directors do not chase or pressure. Urgency is a manipulation tactic.

Requests for inappropriate photos. No legitimate casting for fashion, commercial, or print work requires nude or underwear photos from a new model over unsolicited messages. Ever.

A simple rule from the booking side: at the casting stage, money should always flow toward the model, never away.

What Happens at a Casting Call, Minute by Minute

Here is a typical in-person casting, so nothing in that room surprises you.

Before you enter. You arrive ten to fifteen minutes early, never more. You check in with the casting assistant and fill out a size card: measurements, sizes, agency, and contact. Do not guess your measurements. We check.

The wait. You might wait five minutes or ninety. Stay quiet, stay off speakerphone, and stay ready. Casting assistants notice holding-area behavior, and yes, we hear about it. The model who was rude to the assistant does not get booked, no matter how good the walk was.

Minute one: you enter. You walk in, say hello, and hand over your book or comp card. This is where most of the decision happens, as the next section explains.

Minutes one to three: the book. The casting team flips through your portfolio, usually faster than feels polite. We are checking whether you look like your photos, how your skin, hair, and proportions read in person, and whether your range matches the brief.

Minutes three to five: the walk. For fashion and runway castings you will be asked to walk, usually twice, sometimes in the client's shoes. For commercial castings you may move naturally on camera or try a direction: "Give me relaxed. Now give me energy." Taking direction quickly matters more than nailing it the first time. If your walk needs work, study our breakdown of how models walk in runway shows before your next casting.

Minutes five to seven: digitals. Quick photos against a plain wall: front, profile, hands, and often a short video clip. Neutral expression unless directed otherwise.

Minutes seven to eight: availability. We may confirm your availability for the shoot dates or ask a casual question just to see your personality. Answer honestly. Saying you are available and then dropping out later damages your reputation faster than almost anything else.

Then you leave. Say thank you and go. Do not linger, ask how you did, or ask when you will hear back. The whole meeting often lasts under ten minutes. That is normal, and a short casting is not a bad casting.

What Casting Directors Decide in the First Ten Seconds

A large part of the decision forms before you say a full sentence. Here is what is actually being read in those first seconds.

Do you match your photos? If your digitals show waist-length hair and you arrive with a bob, or your comp card is years out of date, trust is broken immediately. We booked the person in the photos.

How do you carry yourself? Posture, energy, and eye contact register instantly. Not arrogance, and not apology either. The models who book walk in like they belong in the room, because they have decided they do.

Do you fit the brief? Height, proportions, coloring, and overall type get assessed at a glance. This is the part you cannot control, and it is the reason most rejections have nothing to do with your performance.

Would we want you on set for twelve hours? This surprises new models. Clients ask us constantly: "Great, but what is she like to work with?" A pleasant, professional, low-drama presence is a genuine competitive advantage, and we start reading for it the moment you enter.

You cannot fake bone structure, but everything else on that list is trainable. That is precisely the gap our instructors built the Online Model Academy course to close.

What to Wear and What to Bring

The casting uniform exists for a reason: we need to see you, not your outfit.

Wear: Slim-fitting solid basics, typically fitted jeans or slim black trousers with a plain fitted tank or tee. Women should bring heels they can genuinely walk in; men, clean simple sneakers or boots. Minimal or no makeup, hair worn naturally, no heavy fragrance, simple or no jewelry. If the brief specifies something different, the brief always wins.

Bring: Several comp cards, your portfolio if you have one, heels, a phone with your digitals saved, and water.

Do not bring: An entourage. Parents of minors are the exception; anyone else should wait outside the building.

Self-Tape Castings Done Right

Virtual castings are won and lost on fundamentals, not equipment.

  1. Read the brief twice and follow it exactly. If they ask for landscape, film landscape. If they ask for a name slate, height, and location, give them in that order. Deviating from the brief signals you cannot follow direction on set.
  2. Light yourself from the front with window light or a basic light, against a plain, uncluttered wall.
  3. Phone cameras are fine. Stabilize the phone and frame what they asked for: usually full length for the walk, waist-up for the slate.
  4. Dress in the casting uniform unless the brief says otherwise, and look like your current photos.
  5. Keep energy natural. Speak to the camera like a person, not a commercial. Casting teams watch hundreds of tapes; relaxed confidence stands out more than performance.
  6. Check your sound, file name, and deadline. Name the file the way the brief asks, and submit early. Late tapes are usually never watched.

Following Up and Handling Rejection Like a Professional

If you attended through an agency, all follow-up goes through your booker. Do not contact the client or casting office directly. If you attended independently and were told a decision timeline, one short, polite follow-up after that window has passed is acceptable. No response is a response.

Now the part every working model has had to learn. You will be rejected far more often than you are booked, and that is true for successful models too. Remember the brief: most rejections mean you did not fit a picture that was drawn before you walked in. The client wanted a different height, a different coloring, a face that matched the rest of the lineup. None of that is a verdict on you.

What casting directors actually remember is how you show up over time. The model who is professional, punctual, and pleasant at a casting she does not book is the model we call first when the right brief lands on our desk. Rejection handled well is an audition for the next job. That long game, rather than any single booking, is the subject of our guide to building a modeling career that lasts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a model casting call? An audition where a brand, designer, casting director, or agency meets models in person or via self-tape to select who fits a specific job or gains representation. It typically involves a size card, a portfolio review, a walk or on-camera movement, and quick digital photos.

Do you have to pay to attend a casting call? No. Legitimate castings never charge attendance, registration, or consideration fees. Any casting that requires payment or mandatory paid photoshoots as a condition of being booked is a scam.

What should I wear to a model casting call? Slim-fitting solid basics, such as fitted jeans or black trousers with a plain fitted top, minimal makeup, and natural hair. Women should bring heels they can walk in confidently. If the casting brief specifies an outfit, the brief wins.

How long does a casting call take? The meeting itself usually lasts five to ten minutes, though the wait can be much longer. A short casting is normal and says nothing about your chances.

Can you go to casting calls without an agency? Yes. Open calls and many posted castings accept unrepresented models, but agencies receive most requested castings, so getting signed dramatically increases your access to real work.

How do I know if I got the job after a casting? If you booked it, you or your agency will be contacted, sometimes within days, sometimes weeks. There is usually no rejection notice. No response is a response.
