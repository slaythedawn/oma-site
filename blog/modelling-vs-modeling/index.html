Modelling vs Modeling: Which Spelling Is Correct (and Why It Matters in the Industry)

By Online Model Academy · The people who book models · November 2026 · 8 min read

Search for the word and you will find it spelled both ways, sometimes on the same agency website. That is not a typo in either case. "Modeling" and "modelling" are two accepted spellings of the same word, and which one is right depends entirely on where you are and who you are writing for.

Online Model Academy is taught by working casting directors, agents and runway producers from several countries, so we see both spellings every day. Here is the short answer, the reason behind it, and the more useful half of the question: the industry words that really do change from one market to another.

The Short Answer

  • Modeling (one L) is American spelling. Use it in the United States.
  • Modelling (two Ls) is British spelling. Use it in the UK, Australia and New Zealand.
  • Canada sits in between and you will see both, though two Ls is common in Canadian publishing.

Either one is correct. Neither will cost you a booking. If you are applying to an agency, writing a bio or posting your profile, match the market you are targeting and keep it consistent from the first line to the last.

Why the Spelling Differs

It comes down to one small rule. When you add an ending like -ing, -ed or -er to a word that finishes in a single vowel plus a consonant, British English doubles the final consonant and American English usually does not.

That is why the same split shows up in everyday words: traveling and travelling, canceled and cancelled, labeled and labelled. Model follows the same pattern. American English gives you modeling, modeled and modeler. British English gives you modelling, modelled and modeller.

The root word never changes. A model is a model in every country. Only the forms built from it differ, which is why "model agency" looks identical on both sides of the Atlantic while "modeling agency" and "modelling agency" do not.

Which One Should You Use?

If you are applying in the US

Use modeling. Every American agency, casting platform and job listing uses the one-L form, so it is the spelling you will see and the one you should write. If you are searching for agencies, the one-L form is also what turns up the right results. Our guide to how to become a model is written to US spelling for that reason.

If you are applying in the UK or Australia

Use modelling. Agencies, casting calls and listings in both markets use two Ls, and an application written to match looks like it came from someone who knows the local scene. Our market guides for the UK and Australia go through how each market actually works.

If you are in Canada

You can use either, but check the spelling the agency uses on its own site and mirror it. Our Canadian guide covers how that market differs.

If you work across markets

Use the spelling of whoever you are writing to. A model with a UK mother agency and a US market agency will happily write "modelling" to one and "modeling" to the other. Nobody reads it as an error.

Does the Spelling Ever Matter to a Booker?

Almost never. A booker scanning a submission is looking at the photos first, then measurements, then whether you present like a professional. A missing or doubled L is not something anyone books or rejects on.

What does register is carelessness. A submission that is full of errors, or that switches style halfway through, reads as someone who will be hard to work with. So the practical rule is not "get the L right", it is "send something clean and consistent". That is also why the basics in our guide to getting signed by a modeling agency focus on presentation rather than polish for its own sake.

The Terms That Matter More Than the Spelling

If you are planning to work in more than one country, or just researching agencies abroad, the vocabulary differences are worth more of your attention than the extra L. The industry uses different words for the same things in different places.

Runway or catwalk

Americans say runway. In the UK and Australia you will hear catwalk just as often. They are the same thing: the strip models walk down in a show. If you are researching the skill itself, our guide to how models walk in runway shows covers it either way.

Digitals or polaroids

The plain, unretouched photos an agency asks for to see your natural look are usually called digitals in the US. Some agencies and bookers, particularly in Europe, still call them polaroids, a leftover from when they were shot on instant film. They are the same request. Natural light, plain background, no filters, no makeup.

Comp card or zed card

A comp card is the printed or digital sheet of your best photos and stats that a model sends to clients. In some markets you will also hear it called a zed card or z-card, after the folded format. Different name, same job.

Modeling agency or model agency

Both phrases are used, and the second is common in British English. You will also meet the term model management, which means a model agency. When you search for representation abroad, try all three, because a listing may only use one.

Mother agency

This is the agency that develops a new model and manages their career, often placing them with agencies in other cities. The term is used internationally. Our guide to choosing a modeling agency explains when you need one.

Searching for Opportunities Abroad

This is where the spelling does have a practical use. Search engines handle both forms reasonably well, but results are not identical. If you are looking for agencies or casting calls in a specific country, search using that country's spelling and that country's terms. A search for "modelling agencies" and one for "modeling agencies" can surface different lists, and the local form is more likely to return local agencies.

Just as useful: check how an agency describes itself and copy its wording when you write to it. It signals that you have done your homework, which is a small but real advantage when a booker is skimming a full inbox.

A Note on Consistency

If you maintain a portfolio site, a social bio and a set of agency submissions, decide on one spelling per market and stick to it. Mixing both in one document looks accidental. Writing each document in the spelling of its audience looks professional.

The Short Version

Modeling is the American spelling and modelling is the British one, used in the UK, Australia and New Zealand. Both are correct, neither affects whether you get booked, and the right choice is simply the one that matches the market you are writing to. The thing worth learning is the vocabulary: catwalk and runway, digitals and polaroids, comp card and zed card. Know those, send clean and consistent submissions, and you will read as someone who understands the business wherever you apply.
