Building a Model Portfolio Website That Works

By Online Model Academy · The people who book models · August 2026 · 9 min read

Most advice about model portfolio websites is written by people selling website builders. The genuinely useful version starts with a question almost nobody asks: who is going to look at this, and what do they need from it?

Answer that, and the whole thing becomes simple.

Do You Even Need One?

If you are represented by an agency, probably not urgently. Your agency hosts your book, controls how you are presented, and takes enquiries. Clients go to the agency, not to you, and an agency will generally prefer it that way.

A personal site earns its place when:

  • You are freelance and book direct — see our freelance modeling guide.
  • You work across multiple markets with different agencies and want one stable link.
  • You do commercial and brand work where clients research you before approaching.
  • You want a link that does not expire when you change agencies.

If none of those apply, put the effort into your book and your digitals instead.

What It Needs

A homepage that loads instantly and shows one strong image

No splash screen, no autoplay music, no animated intro. A client clicking your link wants to see your face inside two seconds. Anything between them and that is friction.

A curated gallery, 12 to 20 images

This is the whole site, really. Choose images that each show something different: clean beauty, full-length, commercial lifestyle, editorial, and whatever is specific to your board — swim, activewear, parts.

The discipline is in what you leave out. A client scanning 60 photos concludes you cannot tell which of your images are good, which is a worse signal than having fewer.

Your measurements

Height, bust or chest, waist, hips, dress or suit size, shoe size, hair and eye colour. Same data as your comp card, same requirement to be accurate.

A contact route that keeps you safe

Your agency's details if you are represented. A dedicated work email if you are not. Never your phone number, never your home address, and nothing that identifies your suburb, your gym or your regular routine.

What to Leave Out

  • Every photo you have ever taken. Curation is the product.
  • Heavy retouching. Clients book what they see. If you do not look like your site, you will not be rebooked.
  • Personal blog content. Unless you are deliberately building a personal brand, it dilutes.
  • Rates. Rates depend on usage, client and market. Publishing a number either underprices you or ends conversations before they start.
  • Anything locating you. Photos identifying your home, your street or your daily routine.

The Technical Bits That Matter

  • Fast on a phone. Most people will open your link on a phone, often on mobile data. Compress your images properly.
  • Your own domain. yourname.com reads as professional. A free subdomain does not.
  • Correct colour. Export in sRGB or skin tones will shift on other people's screens.
  • Image sizing. Full-resolution files make a beautiful, unusably slow site. Resize before uploading.
  • Keep it current. An abandoned site with two-year-old work is worse than no site.

The platform matters far less than the curation. Any of the mainstream builders will do this well enough.

How It Fits With Everything Else

You end up with four assets doing four different jobs, and confusing them is the most common mistake:

  • Digitals — unretouched phone photos, for agency submissions.
  • Your book — the physical or agency-hosted portfolio, for meetings and castings.
  • Your comp card — the leave-behind at an in-person casting.
  • Your website — the stable link you own, mainly for direct and freelance work.

Our guide to building a modeling portfolio covers the book in detail, and portfolio examples shows what strong selections actually look like.

Start Simple

One page, one strong image, fifteen well-chosen photos, your stats, and a safe contact route. That will outperform an elaborate site with weak curation every time — and it takes an afternoon rather than a month.
