Open Casting Calls: What Really Happens in the Room

By Online Model Academy · The people who book models · August 2026 · 10 min read

Open casting calls are the most accessible route into modeling and the most widely misunderstood. People arrive expecting an audition and get something closer to a measurement queue, then read the brevity as rejection.

Here is what is actually happening, from the people running the room.

Two Different Things Called the Same Name

Agency open calls

An agency setting aside time — often weekly or monthly — to see unrepresented people who might belong on their board. They are assessing potential, not casting a specific job. A yes means they want to talk about representation.

Production open calls

A specific production casting a specific role, often when they want a face they will not find on existing agency boards. These are advertised, time-limited, and assessing you against a defined brief.

The preparation is nearly identical. The mindset differs slightly: at an agency call you are presenting yourself; at a production call you are presenting yourself against a brief you should have read.

What Actually Happens

You arrive. You wait, often for a long time. You fill in a form with your details and measurements. When called, you go in front of one to three people.

Then, typically: they look at you, they may measure you, they photograph you against a plain wall — front, side, sometimes full-length — they ask a few questions, and they say they will be in touch.

Two to five minutes. That is the format working correctly. It is not a bad sign, and reading it as one is the single most common mistake people make.

How You Are Actually Being Assessed

Less on conversation than people assume, and more on things you may not realise are being observed.

  • Do you match a gap? The dominant factor and entirely outside your control. An agency with four similar new faces needs a fifth less than it needs something it lacks.
  • Do you photograph well unstyled? The wall photo is the real test — no lighting, no makeup, no help.
  • Are your measurements what you said? They will check. A discrepancy is a professionalism signal, not a measurement one.
  • How do you carry yourself? Walking in, standing still, being looked at without fidgeting. It reads instantly on camera.
  • Are you easy? Prompt, prepared, pleasant to the person on the door. Bookers ask that person afterwards more often than anyone expects.

What to Bring and Wear

Bring

  • Digitals — four to six unretouched phone photos, daylight, plain wall. Printed and on your phone.
  • Your measurements, written down, in the units of that market.
  • A comp card if you have one.
  • Heels, if you are a woman on a fashion or commercial board.
  • Water, a snack, a pen, and something to occupy a long wait.

Wear

  • Fitted, plain clothing — skinny jeans or leggings, plain fitted top. No busy patterns, no logos, no layers hiding your shape.
  • Minimal makeup. Hair down and natural.
  • Clean, natural nails.
  • Nothing you have to keep adjusting.

The principle behind all of it: the room needs to see you accurately. Anything decorative gets in the way.

The Waiting

Open calls run long. Arriving early is worth it — panels are sharper in the first hours, and being seen before decision fatigue sets in is a genuine, if unfair, advantage.

How you behave in the queue is visible. Staff notice who is pleasant and who is not, and that reaches the panel more often than people realise.

Safety: The Non-Negotiables

Open calls are normal industry practice. Some things that get labelled open calls are not.

  • Never pay to attend. Not a registration fee, not an administration fee, not anything.
  • Never attend at a private residence or hotel room. Studios, agency offices, production spaces only.
  • Always tell someone where you are and when you expect to be done.
  • You may always bring someone. Any objection ends it.
  • Nudity or lingerie must be disclosed in advance, in writing, with usage agreed. Sprung on the day, leave.
  • Verify the caller. If an approach came by DM, confirm through the agency's official published contact details before going anywhere.

Afterwards

Most open calls end in silence, and silence is the normal outcome rather than a personal verdict. Boards fill, briefs are narrow, and timing decides more than anything you did in the room.

Do not chase. One polite follow-up is fine; repeated contact works against you. Go to the next one. Models who get signed are routinely people who attended several open calls over a year or more, including at agencies that had already passed on them.
